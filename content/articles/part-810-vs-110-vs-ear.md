Ask most engineers at a firm that does nuclear work which export rules apply to them, and you'll hear "ITAR, probably?" Usually that's wrong. Civil nuclear exports are split among three regimes run by three agencies. Which one applies depends almost entirely on **what** is moving.

## The short version

| If you're exporting… | Look first at | Agency |
|---|---|---|
| Assistance: technology, know-how, services supporting foreign nuclear activities | 10 CFR Part 810 | Department of Energy |
| Nuclear equipment and material (reactors, especially designed components, nuclear material) | 10 CFR Part 110 | Nuclear Regulatory Commission |
| Dual-use items and technology not covered by DOE or NRC | Export Administration Regulations | Department of Commerce |

The EAR expressly carves out items that fall under NRC and DOE export jurisdiction, so the three regimes are meant to fit together rather than overlap. In practice the boundaries are where people get into trouble.

## Why Part 810 surprises people

Part 810 isn't about shipping hardware. It covers *assistance*: an engineering analysis, a design review, a training session, or a technical conversation with a foreign utility. For an engineering services firm, that means the controlled "export" can be the work product itself.

Some activities are generally authorized, typically involving destinations listed in the regulation's appendix, and they still come with reporting obligations. Others need a specific authorization from the Secretary of Energy, and those can take a long time. Build that timeline into proposals *before* the contract is signed.

## Questions I ask on intake

1. What is actually being transferred: hardware, data, or services?
2. Who receives it, and in what country? Include foreign nationals working here.
3. Does it relate to a "special nuclear material" activity, a reactor, or something supporting one?
4. Is any part of it also defense-related, which would raise ITAR questions?

Answering those four questions honestly settles jurisdiction in most cases. The rest is where you call DOE.
